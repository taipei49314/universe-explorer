## 1. Shape of the domain

| Layer | Claims |

| 🟠 | GW catalogues, spin-powered jets, information paradox |

No confidence percentages. The shape is the content.

## 2. Bedrock: horizons exist

Astrophysical black holes with event horizons are Established because three independent lines converge: horizon-scale imaging of M87* and Sgr A*, gravitational-wave mergers with Kerr-like ringdowns, and stellar orbits at the Galactic Centre (`event_horizon_exists`; Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration, 2019, 2022; Abbott et al., 2016). Horizonless mimickers remain a minority research programme, not a rival textbook consensus.

That floor does not settle quantum gravity. It settles the *astrophysical* claim that nature contains compact objects behaving as black holes.

## 3. Strong shelf: theory, Kerr, and nuclei

Hawking radiation is Strong as a quantum-field-in-curved-spacetime result, with laboratory *analogue* support and essentially no direct astrophysical detection (`hawking_radiation`; Hawking, 1975; Steinhauer, 2016). The evidence-axis divergence—high consensus, weak direct observation—is intentional structure, not a bug.

Astrophysical black holes are well described by the Kerr metric (mass and spin) (`kerr_describes_astrophysical_bh`; Abbott et al., 2016b; Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration, 2019). GW consistency tests and the M87* shadow scale match Kerr within present errors. Parametrised non-Kerr metrics still set limits; they have not replaced Kerr as the working spacetime.

Supermassive black holes commonly inhabit nuclei of massive galaxies (`smbh_common_in_galaxy_nuclei`; Kormendy & Ho, 2013; Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration, 2022). Dynamical masses, scaling relations and the Sgr A* image anchor the local calibration. Seed formation and dwarf-galaxy occupation remain open—those are growth questions, not existence questions for massive systems.

## 4. Yellow: the lower mass gap

Between the heaviest neutron stars and many stellar black holes, a ~2–5 \(M_\odot\) “lower mass gap” is Competing (`lower_mass_gap_compact_objects`; Abbott et al., 2020; Abbott et al., 2023). GW190814’s ~2.6 \(M_\odot\) secondary sits in the disputed window. One camp reads a physical gap from explosion engines; the other reads selection and classification. Cross-link: the *upper* (pair-instability) gap lives under `stars` (`pair_instability_bh_mass_gap`); do not merge the two yellow nodes.

## 5. Orange programmes

Gravitational-wave catalogues of compact binary coalescences are Frontier population science (`bbh_mergers_catalogued`; Abbott et al., 2023): real, rapidly growing, not a finished demographic census.

Powerful jets powered by extracting black-hole spin (Blandford–Znajek–type processes) are Frontier (`jets_extract_bh_spin`; Blandford & Znajek, 1977; Event Horizon Telescope Collaboration, 2021). EHT polarimetry of M87* is consistent with magnetised near-horizon flows used in spin-extraction models; disk winds remain a co-channel. The claim is not “all jets are pure BZ.”

The information paradox remains Frontier theory (`information_paradox`; Hawking, 1976; Penington, 2019; Almheiri et al., 2019): islands and replica wormholes recover unitary Page curves in toy models; the bulk mechanism for realistic evaporating holes is not settled.

## 6. Red ceilings

The firewall is Speculative (`firewall`; Almheiri et al., 2013): a pure clash of assumptions without observational evidence and without mainstream acceptance as a real horizon feature.

*Established* gravitational-wave echoes from reflective horizonless surfaces are likewise Speculative as a discovery claim (`horizonless_gw_echoes`; Abedi et al., 2017; Abbott et al., 2016b). Echo *models* are discussable; community-accepted detections are not. Placing this claim in red prevents a common rhetorical slide from “searches exist” to “echoes are established.”
