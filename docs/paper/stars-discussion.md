## 1. Framing: a green base under yellow and orange structure

The stars domain is not a single light. It is a **shape**: a narrow Established base (core fusion), a broad Strong shelf (nucleosynthesis, remnants, helioseismology, white dwarfs, the local IMF), two Competing nodes (red-supergiant progenitors; Type Ia channels), three Frontier programmes (solar dynamo detail, CNO neutrinos as metallicity probes, the pair-instability black-hole mass gap), and a Speculative ceiling that forbids claiming routine local Population III stars.

We refuse confidence percentages. What the map records is structure you can recount: twelve claims, each hanging on sources that can be opened.

## 2. Bedrock and the solar laboratory

Main-sequence power by core fusion is Established because independent solar-neutrino experiments and nuclear theory converge (`stars_powered_by_fusion`; Ahmad et al., 2002; Fukuda et al., 2001; Bethe, 1939). That claim is the epistemic floor: without it, stellar structure is unmoored.

Helioseismology then turns the Sun into a calibrated laboratory. Global p-modes invert to sound-speed and density profiles that validate standard solar models in bulk (`helioseismology_constrains_solar_interior`; Christensen-Dalsgaard, 2002; Bahcall et al., 2001). The light is Strong, not Established, because the solar *abundance problem*—revised surface mixtures versus the seismic profile—remains an active refinement without displacing the seismic method itself (Christensen-Dalsgaard, 2002; Bahcall et al., 2001).

The solar dynamo that organises the 11-year cycle is Frontier in *detail*: a magnetic engine is the leading framework, but the tachocline, cycle amplitude and grand minima are not uniquely solved (`solar_dynamo_cycle`; Charbonneau, 2010; Hathaway, 2015).

CNO-cycle neutrinos are a different Frontier cut. Borexino’s first detection and later directionality analyses establish that the CNO cycle operates in the Sun at a measurable level (`cno_cycle_solar_neutrinos_detected`; Agostini et al., 2020, 2023). The open programme is precision: whether CNO fluxes can arbitrate core metallicity against the abundance problem, not whether the cycle exists in outline.

## 3. What stars make, and how they die

Most elements heavier than helium are made in stars and stellar explosions (`stellar_nucleosynthesis_makes_elements`; Burbidge et al., 1957; Abbott et al., 2017). The Strong light reflects stable direction with open bookkeeping: relative contributions of AGB stars, core-collapse events and neutron-star mergers remain quantitative research, not a challenge to the stellar origin itself.

Core collapse leaves neutron stars or black holes (`core_collapse_forms_ns_bh`; Baade & Zwicky, 1934; Abbott et al., 2016). White dwarfs are the electron-degenerate endpoints of lower-mass evolution (`white_dwarfs_electron_degenerate`; Chandrasekhar, 1931; Burbidge et al., 1957). Both are Strong: textbook endpoints refined by gravitational-wave catalogues and Gaia sequences, not replaced by rival support mechanisms.

The **red-supergiant problem** is Competing: archival Type IIP progenitors appear to cut off below the highest RSG masses expected from stellar models, and the field splits between physical failed-supernova explanations and calibration or bias explanations (`red_supergiant_problem`; Smartt, 2009; Davies & Beasor, 2020). The yellow light means both camps publish; it does not mean “massive stars never explode.”

## 4. Type Ia channels and the high-mass gap

Type Ia supernovae are cosmologically famous, but their *stellar* question is still yellow. Single-degenerate (white dwarf + non-degenerate donor) and double-degenerate (white dwarf + white dwarf) channels both remain viable at population level (`sn_ia_progenitor_channels`; Maoz et al., 2014). Delay-time distributions, companion searches and CSM constraints have not selected a unique route for normal events (Maoz et al., 2014). Cosmology may *use* standardised Ia luminosities; this domain records that the progenitor engineering is not Established.

At the highest stellar masses, pair-instability physics predicts a gap in black-hole remnants near roughly \(50\text{–}120\,M_\odot\) (`pair_instability_bh_mass_gap`; Woosley, 2017; Farmer et al., 2019). The claim is Frontier: the theoretical gap is articulated; whether current gravitational-wave catalogues show a clean edge or a blurred mixture with hierarchical mergers is an observational programme still under way (Farmer et al., 2019; Woosley, 2017). Horizon-scale *imaging* of supermassive black holes is a different domain (`black_hole`).

## 5. Birth weights and a red ceiling

The stellar initial mass function is approximately universal in present-day star-forming regions (`imf_approximately_universal`; Bastian et al., 2010; Kroupa, 2001). Strong, not Established: extreme starbursts and very low metallicity may still host top-heavy variants, but the local default IMF is the stable working tool of population synthesis (Bastian et al., 2010).

The Speculative ceiling forbids a false comfort: metal-free Population III stars are **not** routinely observed in the local universe (`pop_iii_already_routinely_observed`; Bromm & Larson, 2004). Theory places true Pop III in the early universe; local ultra metal-poor stars are enriched descendants. JWST high-redshift work may yet isolate signatures—that would be a light-moving programme, not a present local census.
