## 1. Framing: a rate, two poles, and no permitted fake precision

The present expansion rate \(H_0\) is not a single laboratory number. It is inferred along routes that encode different physics, different systematics, and different model assumptions. In our knowledge map the **umbrella claim** is Competing: the Hubble constant from the local distance ladder disagrees with the value inferred from the cosmic microwave background (CMB) under flat ΛCDM (`H0_tension_local_vs_cmb`; Riess et al., 2022; Planck Collaboration, 2020; Verde et al., 2019). That light does not mean “cosmology is broken.” It means two high-precision programmes land on incompatible values when each is read in its standard way, and the community has not yet selected a unique resolution.

We refuse confidence percentages. What the map records instead is structure: a high local pole (Cepheid–supernova ladders), a low early-universe pole (CMB+ΛCDM), a second Competing node *inside* the local universe (TRGB versus Cepheids), and three Frontier routes that might eventually arbitrate—early dark energy, strong-lensing time delays, and gravitational-wave standard sirens. The Discussion below follows that order.

## 2. The late-universe pole: Cepheids, supernovae, and SH0ES

Late-universe determinations build a distance ladder: geometric anchors calibrate Cepheid variables; Cepheids calibrate Type Ia supernovae; supernovae in the Hubble flow deliver \(H_0\). The SH0ES programme has pushed this path to high internal precision and consistently reports a local value near \(73\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\) (`shoes_local_H0_high`; Riess et al., 2022). Community reviews place Cepheid–SN ladders among the most mature late-universe routes and document their persistent offset from early-universe inferences (Verde et al., 2019).

This pole is Frontier, not Established, for a deliberate reason. The measurement is real and highly controlled, yet residual systematics—crowding, metallicity, supernova host-mass steps, photometric zero-points—remain actively contested. JWST Cepheid work is precisely the kind of programme that can move the light by changing the zero-point or by failing to do so (Riess et al., 2022). Until independent geometric ladders converge, a high local \(H_0\) is a **result of a method**, not a bedrock fact on the same footing as cosmic expansion itself (`universe_is_expanding`).

## 3. The early-universe pole: the sound horizon under ΛCDM

On the opposite side, CMB experiments do not measure \(H_0\) with a tape measure. They measure the angular scale of the sound horizon at recombination; within flat ΛCDM that scale, together with the baryon and matter densities, implies \(H_0 \approx 67\text{–}68\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}\) (`cmb_lcdm_implies_low_H0`; Planck Collaboration, 2020; Verde et al., 2019). The inference is tight *inside the model*. It is Frontier as a claim about the true expansion rate because the model may be incomplete: any early-universe modification that changes the sound horizon can shift the inferred \(H_0\) without rewriting the CMB power spectrum wholesale.

The epistemic error to avoid is promoting “Planck \(H_0\)” to an Established constant of nature. What is Established is the CMB as hot-big-bang relic radiation (`cmb_hot_big_bang`; Penzias & Wilson, 1965; Planck Collaboration, 2020). What is Strong is late-time accelerated expansion (`accelerated_expansion`; Riess et al., 1998; Perlmutter et al., 1999). What is Competing is the **joint** reading of local and early poles under standard assumptions (Verde et al., 2019).

## 4. A second yellow node: TRGB versus Cepheids

Even before one invokes new physics, the late universe is not unanimous. Tip-of-the-red-giant-branch (TRGB) calibrations of the ladder, notably from the Carnegie–Chicago Hubble Programme, have reported lower local \(H_0\) values than SH0ES Cepheid analyses, easing tension with Planck in some releases (`trgb_vs_cepheid_local_H0`; Freedman et al., 2019; Riess et al., 2022). That split is Competing: two mainstream calibrator hierarchies, both peer-reviewed at high precision, without a decisive community choice.

The importance of this node is strategic. If the local pole itself is still being negotiated, then “new early-universe physics” is not the only live response—and “systematics” is not a vague insult but a named programme of Population I versus Population II standard candles. JWST-era photometry, masers, and eclipsing-binary anchors are the empirical machinery that can collapse this yellow light (Freedman et al., 2019; Riess et al., 2022). Until then, Discussion that treats “the local measurement” as a single number is incomplete.

## 5. Proposed relief: early dark energy

Among early-universe extensions, early dark energy (EDE)—an additional component that contributes appreciably before recombination and then dilutes—has been developed as a concrete way to shrink the sound horizon and raise the CMB-inferred \(H_0\) toward local values (`early_dark_energy_H0_fix`; Poulin et al., 2019; Verde et al., 2019). The proposal is Frontier: rapidly growing, theoretically articulate, and **not** established. Global fits often buy \(H_0\) relief at the cost of tension with large-scale structure or other datasets; no microphysical EDE candidate is uniquely selected (Poulin et al., 2019; Verde et al., 2019).

Honest language matters here. EDE is a **candidate resolution class**, not a discovery. Our map forbids promoting it to Strong or Established because textbooks mention dark energy. Late-time acceleration (Riess et al., 1998; Perlmutter et al., 1999) is a different claim from a pre-recombination EDE episode aimed at \(H_0\).

## 6. Geometric and multi-messenger third routes

Two additional Frontier methods matter because they are not classical Cepheid ladders.

**Strong-lensing time delays.** Monitoring of lensed quasars, combined with mass models of the lens, yields a one-step geometric \(H_0\) (H0LiCOW/TDCOSMO). Published values have often sat nearer the local pole than Planck ΛCDM, while mass-sheet degeneracies and modelling systematics remain under intense scrutiny (`strong_lensing_time_delay_H0`; Wong et al., 2020; Verde et al., 2019). Larger golden-lens samples can move this light; they have not yet ended the tension.

**Standard sirens.** The binary neutron-star merger GW170817 supplied a gravitational-wave luminosity distance and an electromagnetic host redshift, demonstrating a ladder-independent \(H_0\) method (`standard_sirens_H0`; Abbott et al., 2017; Verde et al., 2019). Present posteriors are broad. The method is Established as a *concept* only in the weak sense that the community accepts the measurement channel; as a tension arbiter it remains sample-starved Frontier science. Bright and dark sirens will tighten with the gravitational-wave event rate—descriptively, not as a forecast of which pole wins.

## 7. What would move the lights

| Empirical development | Map consequence (illustrative) |
|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| JWST forces Cepheid and TRGB zero-points into agreement | Collapse or transform `trgb_vs_cepheid_local_H0`; may shift the umbrella |
| Local ladders converge low, matching CMB+ΛCDM | Soften or retire `H0_tension_local_vs_cmb` toward systematics closure |
| Local ladders converge high; CMB+ΛCDM unchanged | Pressure early-universe extensions; keep umbrella Competing until a specific model wins |
| EDE (or similar) fits Planck+BAO+SNe+LSS without collateral damage | Promote `early_dark_energy_H0_fix` only with evidence, never by popularity |
| Time-delay or siren \(H_0\) reaches percent-level and matches one pole | Weight that pole without erasing the other until joint consistency is shown |

None of these rows is a prediction. They are **migration conditions** for an epistemically stratified map (Verde et al., 2019).

## 8. Synthesis

The Hubble tension is best read as a **shape**, not a slogan. Cosmic expansion is Established (Hubble, 1929; Freedman et al., 2001). Accelerated expansion is Strong (Riess et al., 1998; Perlmutter et al., 1999). The CMB is Established as hot-big-bang relic radiation (Penzias & Wilson, 1965; Planck Collaboration, 2020). Against that bedrock, a Competing disagreement between high local ladders and low CMB+ΛCDM inferences remains open (Riess et al., 2022; Planck Collaboration, 2020; Verde et al., 2019), complicated by a second Competing split among local calibrators (Freedman et al., 2019; Riess et al., 2022). Early dark energy, strong lensing, and standard sirens are Frontier tools and proposals—not verdicts (Poulin et al., 2019; Wong et al., 2020; Abbott et al., 2017).

Cross-link to the dark-matter map: the \(S_8\) structure-growth tension (`s8_structure_tension_dark_sector`) is a **different claim**. Both may eventually share microphysics; both may not. Orthogonal treatment until joint evidence forces a link is the disciplined default (Di Valentino et al., 2021; Verde et al., 2019).
