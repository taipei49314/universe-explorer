## 1. Framing: habitability potential is not a biosignature

“Ocean world” is a powerful phrase and a dangerous one. It can mean a geophysical inference—liquid water under ice—or it can be heard as a claim about life. Our map keeps those layers apart. The umbrella claim is Frontier: several icy moons host subsurface liquid-water oceans today (`ocean_worlds_icy_moons`; Kivelson et al., 2000; Thomas et al., 2016). That light records a leading interpretation of magnetic, gravity, and imaging data. It does **not** record organisms.

We refuse confidence percentages and refuse promotional tone. What the structure shows is a cluster of Frontier geophysical and chemical results (Europa, Enceladus, Titan, plume organics) under a Speculative ceiling for extant life (`ocean_world_life_today`). The Discussion follows that order: how oceans are inferred, what Enceladus adds chemically, and what remains forbidden as Established fact.

## 2. The umbrella: multiple moons, remote methods

The inventory claim is deliberately modest. It asserts that *several* icy moons have present-day subsurface oceans, not that every candidate is proven or that properties are known (`ocean_worlds_icy_moons`). The evidence types are geophysical: induced magnetic fields at Europa (Kivelson et al., 2000) and librational decoupling at Enceladus (Thomas et al., 2016). No returned ocean sample exists. That is why the claim is Frontier rather than Established: the interpretation is mainstream and multi-moon, yet still model-dependent and remote (Kivelson et al., 2000; Thomas et al., 2016).

Cross-link honesty: solar-system chronology (`solar_system_age`; Amelin et al., 2002; Connelly et al., 2012) and formation debates are orthogonal. Oceans are a *present-state* claim about interiors, not a claim about how the system formed 4.56 Ga ago.

## 3. Europa: induction as the classic ocean argument

Europa remains the pedagogical archetype. Galileo magnetometer data show time-varying induced fields consistent with a global conducting shell, interpreted as a saline ocean beneath the ice (`europa_induced_field_ocean`; Kivelson et al., 2000; Khurana et al., 1998). Earlier inductive signatures already pointed to a near-surface conducting layer (Khurana et al., 1998); subsequent analyses strengthened the global-ocean reading (Kivelson et al., 2000).

What this does **not** settle: salinity, ice thickness, rock–water contact, or chemistry. Those parameters matter for habitability *potential* and for mission design (Europa Clipper, JUICE), but they are open questions under a Frontier light, not upgrades of the ocean inference into a biosphere claim (Kivelson et al., 2000).

## 4. Enceladus: a plume you can fly through

Enceladus changed the epistemic texture of ocean worlds by offering material to sample in situ in space. Cassini imaged and analysed water-rich jets from the south-polar terrain (`enceladus_plume_global_ocean`; Porco et al., 2006). Separately, measured physical libration requires a decoupled ice shell over a global liquid layer, linking the plume source region to a moon-wide ocean rather than only a local sea (Thomas et al., 2016). The joint picture—active venting plus global ocean—is the leading framework (Porco et al., 2006; Thomas et al., 2016).

Frontier status remains appropriate. Plumbing details, the fraction of plume material that is fresh ocean spray versus ice-shell debris, and the continuity of hydrothermal rock–water interaction are still debated. The plume is a gift to chemistry; it is not a landed ocean laboratory.

## 5. Titan: a deep ocean under an organic-rich surface

Titan’s case is geophysical rather than plume-driven. Cassini gravity measurements of the tidal response imply a decoupled shell over a dense fluid layer interpreted as a subsurface water ocean (`titan_subsurface_ocean`; Iess et al., 2012). Post-Cassini gravity solutions refine the interior model and remain consistent with a deep global ocean (Durante et al., 2019).

Titan forces a second distinction: surface organics and a deep aqueous layer need not be chemically coupled. Dragonfly-era science can probe the surface and atmosphere; the deep ocean remains an inferred, currently inaccessible reservoir (Iess et al., 2012; Durante et al., 2019). Habitability discussions that collapse “organics on Titan” into “life in Titan’s ocean” violate the map’s layering rules.

## 6. Chemistry without organisms: plume organics

Cassini’s Cosmic Dust Analyzer reported macromolecular organic material in ice grains from the Enceladus plume (`enceladus_plume_organics`; Postberg et al., 2018), against the backdrop of the observed plume itself (Porco et al., 2006). That is a **chemical** Frontier result: complex organics in a water-rich, ocean-world environment. It is evidence of interesting prebiotic or abiotic complexity—or of hydrothermal processing—not a detection of living systems (Postberg et al., 2018).

The map’s discipline is sharp here. Upgrading organics to “life found” would be exactly the category error this project exists to block. The open questions are about pathways (hydrothermal versus primordial) and about what would count as a decisive biosignature in a plume or ice shell (Postberg et al., 2018).

## 7. The red ceiling: extant life today

The claim that extant life is present in at least one icy-moon ocean is Speculative (`ocean_world_life_today`). Recorded evidence supports oceans and organics (Kivelson et al., 2000; Postberg et al., 2018); it does not support organisms. Mainstream planetary science does not accept an icy-moon life detection. The red light is not hostility to astrobiology; it is honesty about what has been measured.

What would move this light is not better press language. It is a measurement protocol that the community accepts as decisive against abiotic alternatives—something that has not yet been achieved at Europa, Enceladus, or Titan.

## 8. What would move the lights

| Empirical development | Map consequence (illustrative) |
|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| Clipper/JUICE confirm induction + ice-penetrating radar consistent with a global ocean | Strengthen Europa ocean claims; still not life |
| Plume sample return or landed analysis with community-accepted biosignatures | Could move `ocean_world_life_today` only with extraordinary evidence standards |
| Libration/gravity models falsify a global Enceladus ocean | Demote plume–global-ocean coupling; reassess umbrella inventory |
| Titan gravity/EM consensus against a deep ocean | Demote `titan_subsurface_ocean` |
| Organics shown unambiguously abiotic and non-ocean | Narrow chemical motivation without erasing geophysical oceans |

## 9. Synthesis

Ocean worlds are a **geophysical success story with an astrobiological horizon**. Multiple lines of evidence favour subsurface liquid water at Europa, Enceladus, and Titan (Khurana et al., 1998; Kivelson et al., 2000; Porco et al., 2006; Iess et al., 2012; Thomas et al., 2016; Durante et al., 2019). Enceladus adds sampled organics without sampling organisms (Postberg et al., 2018). The umbrella inventory is Frontier; extant life is Speculative. Missions can thicken the orange layer and—only with rigorous biosignature standards—threaten the red ceiling. Until then, the honest sentence is: **we have strong reasons to infer oceans; we do not have life.**

Cross-link: present-day Mars surface habitability (`mars_sustained_surface_habitability_now`) is a separate Speculative claim about a different body and environment (Wordsworth, 2016; Grotzinger et al., 2014). Do not merge “ocean moons” and “Mars life” into one slogan.
